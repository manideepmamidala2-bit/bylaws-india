## Slide summary

The rules and code stay free and open permanently under Apache-2.0 and CC BY 4.0; income comes from around the data — grants, open sponsorships, firm training, integrations, CSR, and government projects — measured against transparent pilot-year goals.

## Presenter notes

How does Bylaw One stay sustainable while keeping the law free and open? The rules and source code stay open permanently under Apache-2.0 and CC BY 4.0; income comes from around the data. Early support comes from civic-technology grants and openly credited sponsorship of workshops, the student trophy, or a city's rules. Early-to-mid income comes from paid training for firms on reading the rules and using AI responsibly. Mid-term income comes from integrations and hosted endpoints with service guarantees, followed later by CSR funding and government projects built on the same open model. We track pilot-year progress openly, pairing stated goals with live numbers generated from the release.

### Funding options: income from around the data

The rules stay free and open; income comes from around the data:

| Source | When |
|---|---|
| Grants and philanthropy for open civic technology | Early |
| Sponsorship of workshops, the trophy, or a city's rules, credited openly | Early |
| Paid training for firms (reading the rules; using AI responsibly) | Early to mid |
| Services: integrations, hosted endpoints with service guarantees | Mid |
| CSR funding (needs a non-profit with tax registrations and a track record) | Later |
| Government projects on the same open model | Later |
