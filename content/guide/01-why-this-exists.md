## Slide summary

Building rules in India sit across overlapping PDFs from different bodies. Bylaw One turns them into one open, cited, machine-readable code so any person and any AI can answer "what may I build here?" with the official page behind it.

## Presenter notes

Here is the core problem we are solving. Every building in India is governed by rules scattered across bye-laws, master plans, amendments, circulars and state Acts, published as PDFs by different bodies, often contradicting each other. Architects, engineers, students and homeowners spend days finding which rule applies, and still aren't sure. Bylaw One turns those rules into one open, cited, machine-readable code, so that any person, and any AI they use, can answer "what may I build here?" with the exact official page behind every answer. It rests on three ideas: Open, Cited, and Collective — and ends with one habit: Read. Trace. Compare.

## Reading version

> Every building in India is governed by rules scattered across bye-laws, master plans, amendments, circulars and state Acts, published as PDFs by different bodies, often contradicting each other. Architects, engineers, students and homeowners spend days finding which rule applies, and still aren't sure. Bylaw One turns those rules into one open, cited, machine-readable code, so that any person, and any AI they use, can answer "what may I build here?" with the exact official page behind every answer. Built openly, verified by colleges and practitioners, starting with Bengaluru.

Bylaw One is built around three ideas:

- **Open** — free data, free tools, no lock-in to any company, including AI companies.
- **Cited** — every answer leads to the government's own page.
- **Collective** — a city at a time, a state at a time, built and checked by the profession itself.

Closing line: **Read. Trace. Compare.**
