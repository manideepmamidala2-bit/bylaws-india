## Slide summary

Six principles govern every release: verbatim legal text with nothing added inside, every rule traced to its official and archived page, derived values labelled, disagreements settled by stated order, not legal advice, and permanent openness (Apache-2.0 and CC BY 4.0).

## Presenter notes

Every contributor, college steward, and AI integration in Bylaw One is bound by six plain principles. First, the law's words are shown exactly as printed, with nothing added inside legal text. Second, every rule is traced to its official page, with an archived copy. Third, anything the site works out — whether a consolidated provision, an extracted table row, or a 30-metre lake buffer — is explicitly labelled as derived. Fourth, when rules disagree, a stated order decides, and both rules stay visible; we show "not settled" when the law does not decide. Fifth, this is an open reference, not legal advice — always confirm with the authority. Sixth, released data and code stay open permanently under Apache-2.0 and CC BY 4.0.

## Reading version

Six principles govern every dataset, map layer, and interface view in Bylaw One:

- **The law's words exactly as printed; nothing added inside legal text.** Summaries and explanations sit outside the legal text and are labelled `"In short, by this site"` or `"Note by this site"`. Every provision and home-page quote is checked in CI against the extracted text of its cited PDF page.
- **Every rule traced to its official page, with an archived copy.** Every provision, parameter cell, and resolution step links to `#page=N` of the official Gazette, development authority, or municipal PDF alongside a Wayback Machine snapshot and SHA-256 digest.
- **Anything the site works out is labelled as derived.** Consolidated `"as amended"` views, extracted parameter rows, statutory resolution chains, georeferenced land-use zones, and buffer overlays carry `derived: true` and are visibly labelled on the page.
- **When rules disagree, a stated order decides, and both rules stay visible; "not settled" when the law does not decide.** Superseded and municipal provisions remain inspectable under `"Older or replaced rules"`, and genuine ties are shown side by side rather than guessed.
- **Not legal advice; confirm with the authority.** Bylaw One is an open public reference worked out from official documents; building approvals and plot decisions must be confirmed with the planning authority or a qualified professional.
- **Released data and code stay open permanently (code Apache-2.0, data CC BY 4.0).** Underlying statutes, rules, regulations, bye-laws, and gazette notifications are public government works under Section 52(1)(q) of the Indian Copyright Act 1957.
